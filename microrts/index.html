Research study · 2026

Tells Inferring an opponent from play history in a MicroRTS world action model

Andrew Olvera

York College of Pennsylvania

Results

Research question

Does an opponent belief inferred from play history improve a model-based agent under fog of war? Tells separates two tests: predicting future returns and improving actual play. The belief helps the former; the evaluated policies do not establish the latter.

Method

MicroRTS observations are 16×16 categorical boards with visibility masks. The corpus contains 5.93M transitions from 7,215 episodes, split by whole episode and collection seed. About 20% of rows include an engine-cloned counterfactual that changes the agent’s action while holding the opponent’s fixed.

A structured tokenizer encodes game fields, and a flow model predicts latent transitions. A privileged teacher learns 16 local behavior codes; a causal student predicts their distribution from up to 64 frames of visible history and self actions. This belief is denoted z.

Model architecture and data split

Prediction evidence

Tokenizer round trips reconstruct 99.85% of evaluated full-state frames exactly. That does not imply exact dynamics: only 11.7% of frames predicted by the 103.1M-parameter flow model decode exactly, despite one-step changed-cell F1 of 0.973.

Open-loop dynamics

The belief reduces future-return validation loss at 32 and 128 steps. A separately trained state-only head closes much of the gap, and the belief does not improve every return metric.

Control evidence

PMPO policies train on eight-step imagined trajectories in a frozen, ego-observation transition model. This is a separate model from the full-state dynamics diagnostic above. A behavior prior constrains actions; return heads and policy conditioning can receive the belief.

The best no-belief policy recorded 13 wins, 2 draws, and 5 losses in a separate 20-game probe, including 5/5 against CoacAI and 0/5 against workerRushAI. The belief-conditioned policy lost 20 and drew 20 of 40 head-to-head games against it, with strong seat asymmetry.

Interpretation and limitations

The opponent belief contains predictive information, but this study does not demonstrate a control benefit. Five games per bot, a single training seed, and checkpoint selection on the scoring panel make the match results exploratory. Robust superiority across maps, seats, and seeds remains unestablished.

Exact tokenization, accurate one-step prediction, and useful return prediction are distinct requirements. Their individual success does not guarantee reliable imagined rollouts or stronger closed-loop play.
