Starscream Expert-supervised control for drone racing

Andrew Olvera

York College of Pennsylvania

Results

Research question

Can a policy trained on short learner-controlled flight segments complete full courses cleanly and on time? Starscream uses expert action labels at states the learner visits, then evaluates the frozen policy without segment resets or expert assistance.

The comparison covers three approximately 5.84M-parameter policies, with three training seeds each. The transformer setup has the highest nominal clean-and-timely completion rate. Differences in inputs and training budgets prevent attributing that result to attention alone.

Clean-and-timely completion requires finishing in order, without a missed gate, within the course deadline. Eventual completion permits missed gates followed by recovery.

Development evidence. Selection25 is part of this 100-course panel, and both the panel and its 75-course complement were inspected during development. These are not untouched held-out test results. The comparison evaluates complete training setups with different feature handling and budgets, not an isolated architecture intervention.

In the separate 60-start controlled lateral-offset panel, clean-and-timely completion remains near 31–32% for all three setups. Rejoining the first gate does not establish clean full-course recovery. The nominal advantage also does not establish a general lap-time advantage.

Method

A profile-tracking model predictive control (MPC) expert, called MPCC in the implementation, provides labels at visited states. The evaluated mode tracks a course speed profile. Learner-controlled collection segments span two to four accepted gates; replay combines recent data with an expert anchor. Training uses supervised action regression and an auxiliary executed-transition objective, without reinforcement learning.

Policies receive privileged state, three history rows, six upcoming gates, executed-control context, and sampled vehicle parameters. MLP and transformer include explicit approach features that the LSTM lacks. Feature handling and training exposure differ across setups.

Scope and limitations

All results are in simulation with privileged state, exact route geometry, and supplied vehicle parameters. Randomization within the simulator does not establish broad plant generality, vision-only deployment, or physical flight performance.

The development-exposed benchmark, unequal training budgets, and feature differences limit causal interpretation. Three seeds quantify variability within these runs; they do not resolve those confounds. Public reference courses are adaptations, and their timings are not official leaderboard comparisons.
