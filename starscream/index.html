Starscream Sample-efficient generalization for high-speed agile drone racing

Andrew Olvera

York College of Pennsylvania

Paper (coming soon) Results

Abstract

We introduce a hybrid DAgger + DART and dynamics-learning pretraining method for scaling generalization of high-speed agile drone racing with extreme sample efficiency. We also introduce a causal decoder transformer architecture leveraging a sequence design that forces geometric continuous trajectory conditioning. We achieve high accuracy and extreme generalization at high speeds completely from supervised labels produced by an MPCC teacher controller, enabling distillation of privileged, scripted complex behavior at scale.

Observations and tokenization

Starscream is trained in two passes over the same backbone. A privileged teacher sees the simulator’s task state and the route ahead of it, and is supervised directly by an MPCC expert. A vision student is then distilled from the frozen teacher, seeing only a gate segmentation mask and the onboard estimator’s output.

Both tokenize per field rather than per vector. Every field group gets its own SwiGLU projection into d = 320, so a token always means one thing: a state, a control, or one gate in the look-ahead.

Architecture

The actor is a single causal transformer, 5.49 M parameters, reading a 13-token sequence: three steps of state and control history, the six upcoming gates, and one learned readout token. Token identity carries all the position information there is. History tokens get a type embedding and a time index, gate tokens get a learned slot vector per look-ahead index, and there is no other positional encoding and no ALiBi.

That is what forces the geometric conditioning. A gate can only ever be read through the slot for its place in the route, so the continuous trajectory ahead is what the readout has to attend over. Outputs at the first twelve positions are unused; the action and the dynamics prediction both come off the readout token alone.

Observations

Task state in the active gate’s frame, six gates in pass order, and the previous command with its age. Only the current step’s gates are tokenized.

Backbone

Pre-norm blocks, d = 320, 8 heads at 40-d, FFN 640, four blocks, RMSNorm on the output. Causal mask throughout.

Action space

CTBR: collective thrust and three body rates, Gaussian with a state-independent log σ initialized at −1.2 and clamped to [−3.5, 0].

Data & Training

Every label comes from an MPCC expert solved with acados. The expert labels each visited state with the first command of its plan, so the policy learns from the teacher’s intent rather than from whatever the rollout actually executed. That distinction is what lets DAgger, DART noise, and expert-prefix roll-ins all write into the same replay without conflicting.

The run is 218 rounds and roughly 54 M collected steps, with DART and pure expert episodes concentrated in the first four rounds and the DAgger mixing coefficient decaying from 1.0 to 0.35 over the first twenty.

Design decisions

  • The dynamics target is Δs* = s₁MPCC − s₀, the state MPCC predicts after its own first command, not the executed transition. It therefore stays valid when the learner or DART noise chose the executed action, and at gate crossings when the gate frame switches.
  • Only the current step’s gates become tokens. History steps contribute state and control only, which keeps the sequence at 13 tokens while still giving the readout three steps of context.
  • The loss is computed twice, once on normalized CTBR and once in physical units scaled by [10, 3, 3, 3], so thrust error and rate error stay comparable instead of one drowning the other.
  • Replay is mixed by regime, .40 nominal, .35 critical, .25 recovery, and 35% of every batch is drawn from the permanent expert pool so clean expert behaviour never washes out.
  • Solver failures and unsuccessful episodes are dropped rather than labelled, so the student is never asked to imitate a plan the expert could not fly.
