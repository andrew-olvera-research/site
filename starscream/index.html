Overview

Starscream is a learned controller for agile drone racing. The current version separates deciding where to go from working out how to fly there. A feedback controller is pretrained to fly short expert maneuvers, each described by a 64-dimensional motion latent. A planner, whose backbone is first pretrained as a world model, reads the current state and the next two gates and picks that latent at every control tick. Racing reinforcement learning then trains only the planner. It searches among maneuvers the controller already knows how to fly.

About this page. This is a snapshot of the system as implemented on October 27, 2026: the architecture, how it is trained, and recorded flights from the current checkpoint. Systematic evaluation is still underway, so the page reports no benchmark numbers yet.

Skills before strategy

The controller learns from successful MPCC expert trajectories how to turn a motion latent into collective thrust and body rates. It learns this once, and the weights stay frozen afterward.

A better action space for RL

The planner acts in the motion latent, not in raw commands. Reinforcement learning explores flyable maneuvers instead of individual thrust and body-rate values.

Physics before reward

World-model pretraining teaches the planner's backbone how the vehicle moves and responds to commands before any racing reward is involved.

Architecture

Two networks sit in the control loop and share one interface. The planner turns the current observation into a motion latent z. The controller receives that same observation along with z and outputs the command. During controller pretraining, z came from an encoder that could see the expert's next 0.4 s of motion. The planner learns to produce that code from the present observation alone.

What the policy sees

Vehicle state19

Position and velocity relative to the next gate in the body frame, orientation relative to the gate, body rates, and four motor speeds.

Previous command4

The collective thrust and three body rates sent on the last tick.

Next two gates2 × 13

The four corners of each gate, ordered and measured from the drone in its body frame, plus a validity flag.

No observation history. The transformer attends across five tokens from the current tick (state, previous command, two gates, and a readout), not across time.

Training

Each of the five stages trains one piece and hands it to the next. The table shows which network learns in which stage. Expert MPCC trajectories are used during pretraining and alignment. The final stage is pure racing reinforcement learning, with no expert data and no auxiliary losses.

  1. 1Learn the physics

    An action-conditioned world model trains on closed-loop simulated flight. The transformer encodes state, previous command and two gates. It is trained with 32-tick autoregressive latent rollouts and physically scaled losses on state increments, including orientation and rotor dynamics. Only the backbone carries forward. The rollout and reconstruction heads are dropped.

  2. 2Learn an executable motion interface

    An encoder compresses the current observation and eight samples of the expert's next 0.4 s of motion into a bounded 64-D code. The feedback controller sees only the current observation and that code. It is trained with PPO to track the expert, with resets to points along the reference. The expert's future reaches the controller only through the code.

  3. 3Align the planner to the code

    The encoder is frozen and becomes a stop-gradient target. The planner, initialized from the world-model backbone, learns to predict the same 64-D code from the current observation. It has no access to the future. The projector warms up alone before the backbone is unfrozen.

  4. 4Align through the physics

    The planner flies 52-tick windows through the frozen controller and plant. Each window starts from an expert state, then runs on the system's own achieved states, with the observation refreshed every tick. Errors in position, velocity, rotation, body rate and motor speed are measured against the expert at 13, 26 and 52 ticks and backpropagated through the rollout into the planner.

  5. 5Learn to race

    Minibatch PPO trains the planner on a progress, gate-passage and collision reward with a shared critic. A two-gate, four-gate, then full-course curriculum and a learning-progress course sampler spread training across layouts. In the latest continuation, half of the training lanes keep the original seed courses and half fly lightly augmented, geometry-checked variants. The controller stays frozen throughout.

Flights from the current checkpoint

One policy flying eight layouts from its training set with the deterministic (mean) action. They include the exact Swift layout, a reconstruction of the A2RL arena, two MultiGP UTT tracks, and four augmented variants, or grafts, derived from public tracks. The layouts were chosen in advance and recorded without retries. All five recorded runs on each layout were clean.

Off the training set

These two layouts were not in the training set. Both were used earlier in development, so they are examples rather than a held-out benchmark. With the stochastic (sampled) policy, 3 of 17 and 4 of 17 attempts were clean. The videos show successful attempts. Reliable transfer to unseen layouts has not been shown yet.

Simulation setup

All flights use a nominal model of a lightweight five-inch racing quadrotor. It is an engineering model, not identified Swift or A2RL hardware. The policy receives perfect simulator state and an exact gate map, with no observation noise and no dynamics randomization.

Where this stands

Built and running

  • World-model pretraining of the planner backbone
  • Pretrained feedback controller with a 64-D motion latent interface
  • Latent and physical alignment of the planner to that interface
  • Racing PPO with a gate curriculum and adaptive course sampling
  • Clean laps on eight training layouts, including the exact Swift layout and the A2RL arena reconstruction

Not yet established

  • A held-out benchmark with multiple seeds and reported success rates
  • Reliable transfer to unseen layouts (3/17 and 4/17 in the examples above)
  • Ablations showing what each pretraining stage contributes
  • Robustness to sensor noise, latency and vehicle variation
  • Vision-based observations and hardware flight

Research record

Starscream has changed methods several times. Each stage below was published on this site, and the full history is in the site's commit log.

  1. Sep 22, 2026

    Supervised pretraining from an MPCC expert. DAgger- and DART-style imitation with an auxiliary dynamics head.

  2. Sep 23, 2026

    Vehicle randomization and distillation. Randomized vehicles, a commanded-speed input, and teacher–student distillation toward camera-token students.

  3. Sep 30, 2026

    Scaled supervised teacher. A transformer pretrained on 65 qualified courses.

  4. Oct 13, 2026

    Three-seed architecture comparison. MLP, LSTM and transformer controllers that output commands directly. Read the earlier study →

  5. Oct 27, 2026

    Method change: this page. A world-model-pretrained planner over a frozen controller with a motion latent interface, trained with alignment and then racing RL.
